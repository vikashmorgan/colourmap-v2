## The page

One self-contained file, `content/sql/index.html`, served by a route handler like `/coding` and `/git`. It uses School's look: beige paper, a serif text and brown accents, with day and night shared with every School page (`school-theme`).

- **Overall questions and comments** (asked for 2026-10-19). A dashed box above the session buttons, on every tab, for notes about the course as a whole (key `sql|notebook`, the notepad's *The course in general*). It has *Question or comment* and shows its notes like a card, with no dots.
- **One session at a time.** Five session buttons and the cheat sheet, three per row (two on a phone): Session 1 · Relational databases & SELECT, Session 2 · Filter, sort, aggregate, Session 3 · Joins, subqueries, CTEs, Session 4 · KPIs, Tableau, project, Session 5 · Windows, systems, exam. Each session has a thin green bar for how many of its cards are *Got it*. The session last opened is remembered on the device (`sql-session`).
- **Which session you are in** (asked for 2026-10-19). A small *Session n* label (*Cheat sheet* on that tab) sits top left above the title, outside the boxes, and stays pinned to the top of the screen while scrolling; the cheat sheet's search sits just under it.
- **The picture** (asked for 2026-10-19: "a table at the start that gives sense to all the points"). At the top of each session, under the title, a folded-open box *The picture: what the tables look like* draws the session on a tiny made-up shop (4 customers, 5 orders) as real tables. Session 1: a table's rows and columns, primary and foreign key, and SELECT … LIMIT picking columns and rows into a new table. Session 2 (redone 2026-10-20: "more detail, visually, the rows and columns involved"): the query in six steps in run order on a 7-row orders table, each table marking the column the clause reads (a framed band), the columns kept or made, the rows removed and the groups in colour, with a small key: FROM, WHERE status, GROUP BY customer_id then the fold with COUNT and SUM, HAVING, SELECT (why order_id and status disappear), ORDER BY. Session 3: INNER JOIN, LEFT JOIN with the NULL row, and a CTE as a named table. Session 4: the enriched table and the bar chart Tableau makes from it. Session 5: GROUP BY against RANK() OVER (…) on the same four rows, and LAG. Closing it is remembered (`sql-pic-closed`).
- **Cards, in a single column.** Each card has a plain number within its session (15, not 2.15), a name, a sentence or two in plain words, an example query when there is one, and a *Watch out* when the slides flag a trap. Groups follow the decks, and a group about execution order shows the order as a strip labelled *SQL runs it* (FROM → WHERE → …). In Session 2, *The shape of a query* shows two labelled strips, *You write it* (SELECT · FROM · WHERE · GROUP BY · HAVING · ORDER BY · LIMIT) above *SQL runs it*, so the two orders are never confused (2026-10-19). Session 1 has 11 cards, Session 2 has 20, Session 3 has 15, Session 4 has 16 (KPIs, Tableau, the dashboard and the LMDV project) and Session 5 has 17 (window functions, database systems, exam prep).
- **Keyword peek** (asked for 2026-10-19: "when I hover on AS for a second it opens the box of AS on the side, same for CASE WHEN or other similar functions"). Every SQL keyword in a query (SELECT, AS, CASE, WHEN, COUNT, LEFT, OVER, RANK, LAG, strftime…), and the capitalised keywords in a card's text, can be hovered. After 0.6 s its cheat-sheet line opens in a box on the right side: the term, one sentence, the example, and *Session n · open the card ›*. On a phone, a tap opens it as a sheet at the bottom. Escape, × or a click elsewhere closes it. Not inside the cheat sheet itself, where the line is already shown. A keyword's parts lead to the same line (WHEN, THEN, ELSE and END all open CASE).
- **Bold in notes** (asked for 2026-10-19). In any note box (a question, a comment or the notepad), the **B** button at the box's top right, or Ctrl/Cmd+B, makes the selected text bold or plain again. Notes stay plain text: bold is stored as `**word**` and shown in bold in the notes, the notebook and Review, and in answers written from the terminal.
- **Highlights** (asked for 2026-10-19: highlight the lessons "with the nice colour options" of the Python page). Select text on a card, a practice question or answer, the recap or a cheat-sheet line, and a small bar shows the Python page's four light colours (yellow, green, blue, pink). On a card the bar also has *Question* and *Comment*, which open the card's note editor with the passage quoted. Highlights are kept on the device (`sql-highlights`), found again by their text after every redraw, and a tap on one offers *Remove highlight*.
- **Explain more + example** (asked for 2026-10-19: "from points 1 to 15 on session 3 I need an extra explanation and example button, it gets complex"). Session 2's first card (*The full skeleton*, asked for 2026-10-20 in a note on that card: "examples with all of these, visually, rows and columns") has one too: one query using all seven clauses, drawn step by step in the order SQL runs it. WHERE crosses out rows, GROUP BY folds them (each country in its own colour), HAVING crosses out a group, SELECT keeps columns, ORDER BY reorders and LIMIT cuts. Every Session 2 card has one (cards 2–14 and 20 added 2026-10-20, on the same 7-row orders table: WHERE, BETWEEN and IN, LIKE, AND/OR with and without brackets, = NULL vs IS NULL, COALESCE, ORDER BY with one and two keys, aggregates, GROUP BY in colour, grouping by month, WHERE vs HAVING in three steps, CASE, CASE inside SUM, and the quality log). Cards 15 to 19 have one too (asked for 2026-10-20: "develop 15 16 17 18 19"): CASE and GROUP BY (CASE alone, next to COUNT, inside SUM), measuring NULLs (1 or 0 per row, then SUM and the %), finding duplicates (folds of the same email), LOWER/TRIM and a CASE map (seven spellings down to three countries, edge spaces shown as ␣), and the string tools (LENGTH, ||, SUBSTR, LIKE). Each of Session 3's 15 cards has a closed dashed box, *Explain more + example*, under its text. It holds a longer explanation in plain words, the query, and a worked example drawn as small tables on the same tiny shop as The picture: the input, then what the join, filter or CTE makes of it, with matched rows highlighted and dropped rows crossed out.
- **Slides** (asked for 2026-10-19: "add the slides to the lesson pieces"). Each card's foot has *Slide n ›*, which opens that session's deck on the slide the card comes from (`/api/coding/slides/SQL{n}.pdf#page={n}`, the same private route as the Python page). Which slide a card comes from was found by matching the card's words to each deck's text. The decks are the teacher's material, so they are never in this public repository: each user uploads `SQL1.pdf` … `SQL5.pdf` once into their private `coding-slides` folder. The PDFs were made from the class PowerPoints and are named and ready in `Desktop/ADMIN/classes - ALBERTS/SQL/for Colour Brain`.
- **Business case** (asked for 2026-10-20: "the business case SQL as an extra segment in the style of Project 1, and show the functions we learnt in class where they are used, so I can hover and see the box"). The last session button opens Victor's team project for LMDV, *Which venue performed best on Sunday 11 October 2026?* The project's `.sql` file is kept in the page (`CASE_SQL`) with the authors' line removed; the venues and figures are the course's example data, so Victor chose to keep them (2026-10-20, after an upload of the private copy did not work). The page lays it out: the title, the database and the Tableau link, then each STEP as a folded card (the first open) with its explanation. Each query under it has its number and description, the SQL with hoverable keywords, a *Uses* row of chips (each class function once, with the session it was taught in; hover or tap a chip for its cheat-sheet box), and what it found, in a green block. Each step has *Question or comment* and the three dots (keys `sql|case|step{n}`), and appears in the notepad's *About* list.
- **Your slides.** When a deck is missing, a dashed box at the bottom of the page says which sessions, with *Upload slides* (several PDFs at once). Any sensible name works: `SQL3.pdf`, `Session3_….pdf` or `Lesson3_….pdf` is filed as `SQL3.pdf`; a PowerPoint is refused with a note to pick the PDF. It hides once all five are there. Uploads go through `POST /api/coding/slides`. (The bucket also accepts `SQLBusinessCase.sql` as text, migration `0028_slides_text.sql`, from before the case moved into the page; the page no longer uses it.)
- **Each card's foot** is one line: *Question or comment* (a dashed pill, with its count once it holds notes) on the left, and the three dots on the right, big enough to hit in class (40px targets, 20px dots). Each dot's name also shows on tap, so it reads on a phone too; the last one opens leftwards so it is never cut off: *Got it*, *Confused*, *Confused as fuck* (the third, pink-purple dot, renamed from *No time* on 2026-10-19). A dot fills the moment it is pressed. Notes show above the foot, each edited (✎) or deleted (× then Delete?) on its own. An answer written from the terminal shows under its question, with *Got it, mark read*.
- **Practice.** The session's in-class and take-home questions, in plain words, each folded behind *Answer ▸*. The answer is a query and its real output: every query was run on the course database, `shopstream.db`, and the first rows are stored in the page with the row count. Session 1 has 8, Session 2 has 6 plus 6 data-quality audits, Session 3 has 6 plus the 4 lab tasks, Session 4 has 7 and Session 5 has 6 (window functions run on SQLite 3.25+, which DB Browser ships).
- **To remember.** The session's recap in five or six lines.
- **Cheat sheet** (asked for 2026-10-19: "a part where I have all the functions or terms to use in SQL, so I can check it while in class"). A fourth, dashed button after the three sessions. It opens one page with every keyword and function from sessions 1 to 5 (74 lines), grouped in the order a query is written: the skeleton, WHERE, NULL, sorting and summarising, CASE, text and cleaning, tables and keys, joins, then subqueries and CTEs, window functions, KPIs and Tableau, and database systems. Each line has the term, one sentence, a short example when it helps, and a *Session n ›* link that opens its card. A search field stays at the top while scrolling and filters as you type, on every word, through the term, its meaning and its example; *Nothing matches* shows when nothing does. Choosing it is remembered like a session (`sql-session` = `ref`), and the notepad files notes from here under the course in general.
- **The notepad.** The same dot on the right edge and the same beige-and-brown panel as Practice on the Python page. *About* lists the course in general, each session, and each card. It is preselected to the card nearest the top of the screen, and follows the scroll until a place is chosen by hand or a note is being typed. Below, every note grouped by place, *you are here*, and a link that goes to the card (switching session if needed). At its top, a folded **Query order** box (asked for 2026-10-19) shows the two strips, *You write it* and *SQL runs it*, and why the order matters (no aggregate in WHERE, no SELECT alias in WHERE). Open or closed is remembered on the device (`sql-order-open`).

The content is written from the session decks in plain words. The slides themselves stay private and are never committed, and this repository is public.

## Storage

No new table. Marks and notes use the Python page's tables (`coding_marks`, `coding_notes`) through its API, under keys that start with `sql|`:

- a card: `sql|s{session}|{card}`, for example `sql|s2|having`
- a session in general: `sql|s{session}`
- the course in general: `sql|notebook`

The Python page leaves notes with `sql|` keys out of its own lists and Review. The browser copy is written first (`sql-marks`, `sql-notes`). Marks made before signing in are sent up once and never overwrite the account. Served outside Colour Brain, the page works on its own in the browser. Answers can be written with `scripts/coding-answer.ts`, like the Python questions.

## Access

`/sql` redirects a logged-out visitor to `/login?next=/sql`. The School menu's SQL card, until now *Coming soon*, opens it.
